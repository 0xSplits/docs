# Arc

Splits V2.2 (Push and Pull splits) and the Warehouse are deployed on Arc
mainnet (chain id 5042) and Arc testnet (chain id 5042002).

Arc-owned facts, such as how USDC works as the native token, fees, and RPC
behavior, live in [Arc's docs](https://docs.arc.io/arc/references/evm-differences).
This page only covers how Splits behaves there.

## What is deployed

Only the Warehouse and SplitV2 (Push and Pull splits) are deployed on Arc. Split
V1, V2.0 and V2.1 splits, Waterfall, Swapper, Oracle, Pass-Through, Vesting,
Liquid, Recoup and Diversifier are not.

Addresses are listed in the "Arc – 5042" sections of the
[Warehouse](/core/warehouse) and [SplitV2](/core/split-v2) pages. They are the
same on testnet, listed under "Arc Testnet – 5042002".

## SDK

Arc requires `@0xsplits/splits-sdk` 6.7.0 or later, and
`@0xsplits/splits-sdk-react` 4.6.0 or later if you use the React hooks.

On Arc the SDK supports only the [SplitV2 client](/sdk/splits-v2) and the
[Warehouse client](/sdk/warehouse). The data client, and the subgraph behind it,
do not cover Arc, so you do not need an API key for Arc. When `distribute` needs
a split's recipients and you have not passed them, the SDK recovers them from
onchain logs through your provider.

## USDC on Arc: two token ids

On Arc, USDC is the native token. Arc exposes it two ways, and both views read
the same balance (see [Arc's docs](https://docs.arc.io/arc/references/evm-differences)):

- **Native**, with 18 decimals.
- **ERC-20**, at `0x3600000000000000000000000000000000000000`, with 6 decimals.

Splits treats these as two different token ids, as the
[Warehouse](/core/warehouse) does for any two tokens:

- The native id is `0xeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeee`, the same id the
  Warehouse uses for ETH on other networks.
- The ERC-20 id is the `uint256` form of `0x3600000000000000000000000000000000000000`.

What follows from that:

- **The funding route does not matter.** After you fund a split with USDC by
  either route (an ERC-20 transfer or a native value send), the split's balance
  is visible under both ids: 100 USDC reads as `100000000` under the ERC-20 id
  and `100000000000000000000` under the native id. Never add the two together.
- **`distribute` credits under the id you pass.** Recipients are credited under
  that id, whichever route funded the split. `withdraw` is also per id, so a
  recipient credited under both ids makes two withdrawals, and withdrawing under
  an id with no credit reverts.
- **The Warehouse labels both ids the same**: the name is "Splits Wrapped USDC"
  and the symbol is "splitsUSDC". Only the decimals differ.

How a distribution behaves depends on the id and on whether the split is Push or
Pull:

| Route | Token id | Decimals | Push behavior | Pull behavior | Failure modes |
| :---- | :------- | :------- | :------------ | :------------ | :------------ |
| ERC-20 | `0x3600…0000` | 6 | `distribute` pays recipients directly. A blocklisted recipient makes the whole call revert and nobody is paid; so does a blocklisted distributor when the distributor fee is above 0. Contract recipients that revert on receive are fine. | `distribute` credits the Warehouse and succeeds. Only a blocklisted recipient's own `withdraw` reverts. A contract that reverts on receive can still withdraw. | On an immutable Push split, a blocklisted recipient blocks distribution permanently. |
| Native | `0xeeee…eeee` | 18 | A recipient that cannot take native value (a blocklisted address, or a contract that reverts on receive) does not block the others. Its share is credited in the Warehouse under the native id. | `distribute` credits the Warehouse and succeeds. Only the affected recipient's own `withdraw` reverts. | A share credited to a recipient that cannot receive stays stuck until that recipient can receive again. A distributor that reverts on receiving a reward above 0 reverts the whole Push call. A recipient contract can also block a Push distribution by re-entering `distribute`; see the notes on the [SplitV2](/core/split-v2) page. |

Real transactions on Arc mainnet confirmed that funding by either route is
visible under both ids, that `distribute` credits only the id you pass, and that
`withdraw` pays out per id. They used four splits of 0.1 USDC each (Pull and
Push, funded by ERC-20 transfer and by native send). The failure modes in the
table come from read-only simulations (`eth_call` with state overrides, nothing
broadcast), using an address that is blocked on Arc testnet. The ERC-20 id is
served by an upgradeable contract controlled by Circle and Arc, so this behavior
can change.

### Recommendation

This is guidance derived from the behavior above, not a protocol rule.

- Default to the ERC-20 id. It matches Arc's guidance of using the 6-decimal
  interface for transfers and accounting, and it works for any contract
  recipient.
- On the ERC-20 id, use a Pull split when recipients are many, untrusted, or
  could be blocklisted. Use Push only for a few known recipients and an owner who
  can remove one. Never use an immutable Push split if a recipient could be
  blocklisted.
- Pick one id per split and stay with it. When you work on a split you did not
  create, check both ids.
- Use the native id only if you specifically need Push distribution to keep
  working when one recipient cannot receive, and you accept the stuck credit
  described above.

<Callout type="warning" emoji="⚠️">
**The zero address is not USDC in the SDK.** `distribute` and
`WarehouseClient.withdraw` treat a zero `tokenAddress` as the native id, but
`getSplitBalance` does not: it passes the address through as given. Pass the
ERC-20 address explicitly in all three.
</Callout>

## Distributing and withdrawing

`distribute` needs the split's full configuration, and a mismatch reverts with
`InvalidSplit()`. The configuration is emitted in the `SplitUpdated` event, at
creation and on every update. The split's `updateBlockNumber()` returns the
block of the latest one, so you can read that single block instead of scanning
history. In the SDK, you can skip recovery by passing `splitFields` (the
`recipients` and `distributorFeePercent`) to `distribute`.

The distribution incentive goes to the distributor address passed to
`distribute`, which the SDK defaults to the connected wallet. On a Pull split it
is credited to that address's Warehouse balance, so it needs a `withdraw` too.

`distribute` does not pay out everything. It keeps 1 unit in the split, a Pull
split also keeps 1 unit in its own Warehouse balance, and rounding remainders
stay behind. The Warehouse's `withdraw(owner, token)` pays the balance minus 1:
it pays 0 at a balance of 1 and reverts at a balance of 0. The unit that stays in
a recipient's Warehouse balance is included in their next withdrawal. One unit
is 0.000001 USDC under the ERC-20 id, so smaller amounts can only move through
the native id, and 1 wei under the native id.

For example, a Pull split with three recipients (50/30/20), funded with 0.1 USDC
and distributed under the ERC-20 id, credited 49,999, 29,999 and 19,999 units.
After their withdrawals the recipients had received 49,998, 29,998 and 19,998
units, and 6 units of the 100,000 were left behind: 1 in the split, 2 in the
split's Warehouse balance, and 1 in each recipient's Warehouse balance.

## Network notes

| Network | RPC |
| :------ | :-- |
| Arc – 5042 | `https://rpc.mainnet.arc.io` |
| Arc Testnet – 5042002 | `https://rpc.testnet.arc.io` |

- **`eth_getLogs` range.** On the public RPCs for both networks, a range of
  10,000 blocks works and 10,001 fails with error `-32012` ("requested range too
  large"). The public RPCs also rate-limit requests (error `-32005`). Use
  `updateBlockNumber()` to avoid wide log scans.
- **Fees and logs.** Arc sets a minimum fee per gas. A USDC move through the
  ERC-20 interface emits two `Transfer` logs, and a native move emits one. Both
  are defined by Arc, not Splits. See
  [Arc's docs](https://docs.arc.io/arc/references/evm-differences) for the
  current fee requirements, and
  [USDC system events](https://docs.arc.io/arc/references/usdc-system-events)
  before you count `Transfer` logs.
- **Testing.** A local fork does not reproduce the behavior of the upgradeable
  ERC-20 contract at `0x3600…0000`, so test with `eth_call` against the Arc RPC.
